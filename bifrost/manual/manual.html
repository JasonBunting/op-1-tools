Bifrost a bridge for hundekraftORIGINAL OP–1 · HUNDEKRAFT 3.3.2 · BIFROST 0.2.0
BIFROST / MANUAL

Your keyboard, straight in.

Bifrost lets you plug a USB MIDI keyboard or controller straight into the OP-1, with no computer in between, and play it. The OP-1 powers the keyboard from its battery and plays its notes as it plays MIDI from a computer.

What you need

  1. An original OP-1 running Hundekraft 3.3.2, with Bifrost installed.
  2. A mini-USB OTG adapter or OTG cable: a mini-USB plug on one end, a full-size USB socket on the other.
  3. A class-compliant USB MIDI keyboard or controller: one that works on a computer without installing a driver. Most do.
Warning

It has to be a real OTG adapter. Inside the plug, an OTG adapter connects the mini-USB plug's fifth pin (ID) to ground, and that is what tells the OP-1 to be the host. Many adapters sold as "mini USB OTG" are plain plugs that look the same and do nothing. The cable that came with the OP-1 is not an OTG cable.

Get started

  1. Install bifrost.knl from the Hundekraft workshop's Your plugins page. Building it is described in the Bifrost README. The OP-1 restarts.
  2. Unplug the computer's USB cable from the OP-1.
  3. Plug the OTG adapter into the OP-1 and the keyboard's USB cable into the adapter. The order doesn't matter.
  4. Within about 5 seconds the keyboard lights up and a pop-up shows its name, such as MPK mini 3 connected.
  5. Play.

Playing

The OP-1 treats the keyboard exactly as it treats MIDI from a computer, so everything the OP-1 and Hundekraft do with MIDI works: velocity, chords, the sequencers, recording to tape, and Hundekraft's MIDI features. Teenage Engineering's guide covers how the OP-1 handles MIDI: the OP-1 guide.

Bifrost works whatever sound or mode you are in. Pick sounds on the OP-1 as usual and play them from the keyboard.

Tip

If the keyboard lights up and "connected" shows but nothing plays, check its MIDI channel against the OP-1's. Setting the keyboard to channel 1 is the quickest test.

Knobs: your controller as the OP-1's encoders

If your controller has knobs, Bifrost can make them turn the OP-1's four encoders. Eight knobs work best: the first four turn blue, green, white and orange on page 1, and the next four turn the same encoders on page 2. A controller with four knobs gets page 1 only.

The knobs turn the encoders, they don't set values. So nothing jumps when you change sounds: a knob always moves a value from wherever it is, as the OP-1's own encoder does. That works on every screen: the engine, the envelope, the effects, the LFOs and the mixer.

Learning your knobs

Bifrost doesn't know your controller's knobs until you show it, once:

  1. Plug the controller in, so ... connected shows.
  2. Hold Shift and press , then T3. Hold Shift and turn blue to the BIFROST page.
  3. Set LEARN KNOBS on. A pop-up asks for the top row's left knob.
  4. Turn each knob a little, one at a time, in this order: the top row left to right, then the bottom row left to right. After each, a pop-up names the next one.
  5. After the eighth, 8 knobs learned shows and LEARN KNOBS goes off by itself. With fewer knobs, set it off yourself after the last one.

Bifrost remembers the knobs across restarts. It also works out by itself whether each knob sends a position or a change, so either setting on the controller works. If yours offers a "relative" mode for its knobs, use it: a knob that sends positions stops at its ends, while a relative one can keep turning.

Page 1 and page 2

On a plugin engine's main page, pushing an encoder switches it to its page 2. Your controller's knobs can't be pushed, so Bifrost pushes for you: turn a bottom-row knob and its encoder switches to page 2 first, then turns. Turn a top-row knob and it switches back. You see the page change on the OP-1, as if you had pushed the encoder.

Note

Some engines use an encoder's push for something else, a freeze switch for one. When a push doesn't switch the page, Bifrost pushes once more straight away, which puts that switch back, and the bottom-row knob turns page 1 instead until you change sounds.

To turn the knob map off and let your controller's knobs send plain MIDI, set KNOB MAP off on the BIFROST page.

Plugging and unplugging

  1. Unplug the keyboard from the adapter at any time. A pop-up says it was unplugged. Plug it (or another one) back in and it connects again within a second or two.
  2. Take the adapter out of the OP-1 and the USB port goes back to normal within a few seconds: plug the computer in and the workshop, disk mode and Mirror work as usual.
  3. Disk mode (Shift + COM, then T3) needs the computer, so starting it ends Bifrost's host mode.

Turning it off

  1. Hold Shift and press , then press T3. The settings page opens.
  2. Hold Shift and turn blue until the page titled BIFROST shows.
  3. Set USB KEYBOARD off.

Off, Bifrost does nothing at all and the USB port behaves exactly as on a stock OP-1. Turn it back on the same way.

Messages

MPK mini 3 unpluggedIt was unplugged. Plug it back in when you like.
...: not a midi deviceA flash drive, a mouse or anything else that isn't USB MIDI. Bifrost leaves it alone; unplug it.
...: usb setup failedThe device didn't answer as expected. Unplug it and plug it in again.
bifrost: no 5v on the usb portThe OP-1 couldn't power the port. Bifrost tries again after 30 seconds.

Limits

  1. Notes go one way, from the keyboard to the OP-1. Nothing is sent back to the keyboard.
  2. The knob map is for knobs. Faders, pads and buttons send their MIDI as usual.
  3. One device at a time, plugged in directly. USB hubs aren't supported.
  4. While the adapter is in, the OP-1 powers the port from its battery and doesn't charge. Take the adapter out when you're done.
  5. Only USB MIDI devices: no flash drives, mice or audio.

Where it comes from

The OP-1's hardware can be a USB host, but its stock firmware never uses that. How the OP-1 switches its port into host mode and powers it was worked out from the stock firmware and from how Opus, LizardMan's OP-1 firmware mod, does it on the same hardware. Bifrost's code is its own.
