RATATOSKR / MANUAL

Up and down the tree.

Ratatoskr is a synth engine that turns a recording into a cloud of tiny overlapping grains. A playhead scurries through the recording, slow enough to stretch a word into a pad, fast enough to scatter it, forwards, backwards or frozen still, and it can drift, leap, turn and bounce about by itself. Every key plays the cloud at its own pitch.

About this manual

Ratatoskr is a synth engine, so everything the OP-1 does around an engine still works as usual: the envelope, the effects, the LFOs, the octave, and saving sounds. Teenage Engineering's guide covers those: teenage.engineering/guides/op-1/original/synthesizer-mode. This manual covers Ratatoskr's own knobs, screen and playhead.

How to read the controls

The OP-1 has four encoders. This manual calls them by colour, left to right: blue, green, white and orange.

  1. Turn an encoder to change a value. Push an encoder until it clicks to press it like a button.
  2. Press a key, or hold it while you do something else. "Shift + T1" means hold Shift and press T1.
  3. Text in a black box, such as speed +0.25x, is what the screen shows.

Screens have numbered dots. The list beside the picture says what each number shows. Dot colour matches the encoder that changes that part.

Get started

  1. Install ratatoskr.knl from the Hundekraft workshop's Your plugins page. Building it is described in the Ratatoskr README.
  2. Press and pick a sound with a sound key, 1 to 8.
  3. Hold Shift and press T1. Turn blue to ratatoskr and press T1.
  4. Record something into it, as in Recording: press the input key, hold any key while you sing or play into the mic, and let go.
  5. Hold a key. You hear a soft, smeared version of your recording, creeping forwards through it at a quarter of its speed.

Recording

Ratatoskr plays the sound's own sample: the same audio the stock sampler engine would play on that sound, up to six seconds, mono. Record it with Ratatoskr selected:

  1. Press the input key (top right, the microphone symbol). It records from the OP-1's selected input source, as the sampler does; TE's guide explains choosing the mic, line in or the radio.
  2. Hold any keyboard key and sing, speak or play. Let go to stop.
  3. The sound stays on Ratatoskr, and the new recording appears across the top of the screen.
Warning

Record with Ratatoskr selected rather than in the sampler engine before switching. When a sound switches to Ratatoskr, the OP-1 loads that sound's sample from its disk, which can be an older recording than the one you just made.

Material with movement in it works best: a sung phrase, a few chords, a spoken sentence, a drum loop. A single steady tone sounds much the same wherever the playhead is.

Loading a saved sample

Push orange to open the sampler's sample browser. It lists the sampler presets on the OP-1; pick one and press white to load its sample into the sound. The sound stays on Ratatoskr with its own settings.

The screen

A knob on its second page shows its page-2 name and a second line above its colour. With nothing recorded on the sound, the screen says record a sample first and the keys are silent.

Grains

A grain is a short slice of the recording that fades in and out smoothly. Ratatoskr starts new grains all the time, each taken from around the playhead, and overlaps them into a continuous cloud. Page 1's green and orange, and page 2's blue, shape the grains:

KnobDoes
green sizeHow long each grain is, 10ms to 500ms. Short grains buzz and blur the sound into texture; long ones keep words and notes recognisable
orange densityHow much the grains overlap, 0.5x to 4.4x. Below 1x there are gaps between them; high values make a thick, smooth cloud. The level is evened out as it rises
blue page 2 sprayHow far from the playhead each grain may start, up to 1.5 seconds either way, at random. At zero every grain starts at the playhead; turned up, the cloud mixes moments from all around it

The playhead

The playhead is what moves. It travels through a stretch of the recording, the region, at the speed white sets, and the grains follow it. It is shared by every key you hold, and it only moves while something is playing, so each new note carries on from where the last one left off.

The region

  1. blue start sets where the region begins, shown in seconds into the recording.
  2. green page 2 width sets how far it reaches to the right, from 0% to the whole recording. The start stays put; only the end moves, and it stops at the recording's end.
  3. At width 0 the playhead sits on the start and doesn't move, whatever the speed: turning blue then scrubs through the recording by hand.
  4. When the playhead reaches the end of the region it wraps round to the start, and the other way round when it runs backwards. With edges set to bounce (Shift + white) it reverses instead.

Speed and stretching

white speed is centred: in the middle it reads frozen and the playhead stands still. Turn right to go forwards and left to go backwards, up to 4.00x either way. +1.00x, three quarters of the way to the right, moves through the recording at its own speed.

The speed of the playhead and the pitch of the grains are separate. Below 1x the recording is stretched out in time without dropping in pitch: at +0.25x a one-second word takes four seconds. Above 1x it is squeezed, still at the same pitch. The pitch comes from the key you play.

Tip

Most of white's turn is given to the slow speeds, where stretching happens. Frozen with a long grain size and high density gives a held, still pad of one moment of the recording.

Wander: drift, jumps, turns, edges and chaos

Five settings let the squirrel off its line. Drift is on white's page 2. The other four are on Shift + turn: hold Shift and turn an encoder. They move one step per click, are saved with the sound, and show across the top of the screen, each above its encoder in its colour, for example jumps 30%. The one you changed last is brighter and underlined.

ControlDoes
white page 2 drift0 to 100%: the read point (the thin white line) wobbles around the playhead, up to a quarter of the region either way, changing aim every second or so
Shift + blue jumps0 to 100% in 10% steps: now and then the playhead leaps to a random place in the region, up to about once every two seconds
Shift + green turns0 to 100% in 10% steps: now and then it turns round and runs the other way, up to a little under once a second
Shift + white wrap / bounceWhat happens at the region's ends. wrap: the playhead jumps back to the other end and carries on the same way. bounce: it reverses, ping-ponging between the ends
Shift + orange chaos0 to 100% in 10% steps: jumps and turns stop coming at a steady random rate and arrive in bursts and lulls instead, with a new mood every second or so. On average they come about as often as without chaos

Low drift gives a gently restless texture; jumps and turns scatter the recording into an ever-changing collage. Jumps and turns stay inside the region, so narrow the region to keep the wandering on one part of the recording. A new sound starts with all five at zero and wrap.

Playing it

  1. Each key plays the cloud at its own pitch. C4 (the middle C at the normal octave) plays the recording at its own pitch.
  2. Six keys at once, as every OP-1 synth engine. Every key reads from the same playhead, so a chord is one cloud at several pitches.
  3. Ratatoskr uses the OP-1's own envelope (T2). A slow attack and a long release make it a pad; a short decay turns each note into a burst of grains.
  4. The OP-1's effects (T3) suit it well: a delay or a spring reverb spreads the cloud further.

With the OP-1's LFOs

The OP-1's LFOs can turn Ratatoskr's page-1 knobs for you. On a synth sound, T4 switches the LFO on and off, and Shift + T4 picks its type. Each type uses the four encoders differently (from TE's guide):

LFOEncodersWith Ratatoskr
Valuespeed amount destination parameterDestination the synth, parameter blue: the region slides back and forth through the recording. With width at 0 this scrubs, like rocking a tape reel by hand. On white, the playhead swings between forwards and backwards
Elementsource amount destination parameterSource the g-force sensor, parameter blue: tilt the OP-1 to move through the recording. Source the envelope, parameter green: grains grow longer as each note opens
Randomamount speed destination attack/decayMoves start, size, speed and density all at once. Keep the amount small, or the cloud changes character constantly

The LFOs reach page 1 only. The white line and blue arrow on the screen show where an LFO has moved the playhead.

Recipes

When a recipe works on a recording, save the sound as TE's guide describes; the sound keeps Ratatoskr's settings.

Limits

  1. Six voices, as every OP-1 synth engine, with up to six grains each.
  2. Recordings up to six seconds, mono, as the OP-1's sampler.
  3. The playhead moves only while a key sounds; between notes it waits.
  4. The OP-1's LFOs reach the page-1 knobs, not spray, width, drift or the Shift settings.
  5. The Shift settings need a core that saves engine records (Hundekraft 3.3 and later do). Older cores refuse the plugin.
  6. Velocity sets the level only when notes arrive with velocity, such as over MIDI: the original OP-1's keys always play at full.

Where it comes from

Ratatoskr is named for the squirrel of Norse myth who runs up and down Yggdrasil, the world tree, carrying words between the eagle at its top and the dragon Níðhöggr gnawing at its roots. Here the tree is your recording, and the squirrel is the playhead.

Ratatoskr or Hundekraft's granular?

Hundekraft comes with its own granular engine, which also plays grains from the sound's recording. They differ in what moves:

granularRatatoskr
Where grains come fromOne fixed point, until you turn blue or an LFO doesA playhead travelling through a region, frozen to 4x, forwards or backwards
Stretch, reverse, wanderingNoYes: speed, wrap or bounce, drift, jumps, turns, chaos
KeysEach key separateOne shared playhead for every key
Grain shapesSix, including pluck and swellOne, smooth (Hann)
Pitch and gain pages±24 semitones; 0–4x gainNone
DetuneA little per grain with sprayNone

Load on the OP-1LighterHeavier, but six keys at full density play cleanly

Use granular to shape grains from one spot: percussive or swelling grain shapes, a pitch or gain page, or the lightest load. Use Ratatoskr for textures that travel through a recording, stretch it out or run it backwards, and keep changing while you hold a note.

The idea of a roaming playhead comes from GrainBrick, a granular instrument for the TrimUI Brick handheld. The pitch constant, the level for overlapping grains and the way the recording's length is found follow Hundekraft's own granular engine, which plays grains without a moving playhead; Hundekraft's clouds and hail are granular effects.
