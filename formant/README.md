# formant

A vowel synth engine for the original OP-1 running Hundekraft 3.2.3. Each note is a vocal-fold pulse shaped by
five formants (the resonances that make "ee" sound like "ee"). The formant frequencies come from
[CHIMERA](https://github.com/jamesondh/chimera-vocal-synth)'s vocal-tract model, for a man, a woman and a child.
Six-voice polyphony. A TALK mode turns held notes into made-up syllables; there are no real consonants.

Build with `tools/build.sh formant`, which makes `build/formant.knl`.

| File | What |
| --- | --- |
| `plugin/formant.c` | The engine (knobs, voices, level) and its screen. |
| `plugin/formant.svg` | The screen's SVG: empty, since `formant.c` draws everything. |
| `plugin/fm_dsp.h` | The pulse, the five resonators, vibrato and drift. Integer only. |
| `plugin/fm_tables.h` | Formants for nine vowels and three bodies, generated by `plugin/gen_tables.py`. |
| `plugin/test/host_test.c` | Host test: coefficients, stability, loudness; writes WAVs to listen to. |

Host test, in WSL from the repository root (the WAVs go to `build/`):

```bash
gcc -O2 -Wall -I formant/plugin formant/plugin/test/host_test.c -lm -o /tmp/fm_test && /tmp/fm_test build
```

To regenerate the table, download CHIMERA's `crates/chimera/src/phonetics/vowelTargets.generated.json` and run:

```bash
python formant/plugin/gen_tables.py path/to/vowelTargets.generated.json
```

## Screen

1. **Top:** a mouth: wide for "ee", round for "oo", open for "ah", and bigger for a bigger body. It follows the first sounding voice and closes on
   each talk syllable; with no key held it follows the knobs.
2. **Middle:** a vowel strip from "ee" (left) to "oo" (right). The blue mark is blue's place; a white dot shows each
   sounding voice's vowel, so in talk mode you can watch the dots hop.
3. **Bottom:** each knob's name and value in its colour. A knob on page 2 shows its page-2 name and a second line
   above its colour line.

## Controls

Blue, green and white each have a second page: press the knob until it clicks to switch it, press again to switch
back. The screen's bottom row shows which page each knob is on. Page 2 knobs move in steps, one per click of the
encoder.

1. **Blue: vowel.** Fully left is "ee" (heed), then "i" (hid), "ay" (hayed), "e" (head), "a" (had), "ah" (hod,
   about two-thirds right), "aw" (hawed), "oh" (hoed), and fully right is "oo" (who'd). It glides smoothly between
   them. Pointing an LFO at blue works well.
2. **Green: body.** Fully left is bigger than a man, a sixth of the way a man, halfway a woman, five-sixths a
   child, and fully right smaller than a child. This moves the formants only, not the pitch.
3. **White: breath.** Adds air; fully right is a whisper.
4. **Orange: vibrato.** Left half: depth, up to about a semitone each way, fading in over a quarter second after
   each note starts. Right half: the vibrato speeds up into a warble. Every note also wanders slightly in pitch,
   differently in each voice, so chords sound like a small choir.

**Page 2: TALK.** Turns a held note into made-up syllables: the vowel hops around the blue position at a speech-like
rhythm, and each syllable can start with a quick "m" / "w" / "b"-like closing. The syllables are chosen by the key,
so the same key always "says" the same word and another key says another.

1. **Blue (press): talk rate.** "off" (a plain held vowel), then nine steps from 1.5 to 10 syllables a second.
2. **Green (press): spread.** How far the syllables wander from the blue vowel, 0–100% in steps of 10: low keeps
   them close (mumbling around one vowel), high uses all of them. Even 0% wanders a little.
3. **White (press): chop.** How strongly each syllable starts with a closing, 0–100% in steps of 10: low is smooth
   vowel flow, high is choppy. Even 0% closes a little, so talk is always audible when it's on.

## Device test

Install `build/formant.knl` through the workshop as usual (it replaces the earlier version). Talk is on by default
for a newly chosen engine; a sound slot set up with an earlier version may start with talk off (the screen shows
"off" under "talk" on blue's page 2).

1. **It plays.**
   1. Press the synth key, hold Shift and press T1, and choose **formant**.
   2. Expect a new screen: a mouth at the top, a strip marked "ee", "ah", "oo" in the middle, and four labels at
      the bottom: "vowel", "body", "breath", "vibrato".
   3. Hold a key in the middle of the keyboard. Expect a voice babbling made-up syllables around "ah", the mouth
      opening and closing, and a white dot hopping along the strip.
2. **Talk.**
   1. Press blue until it clicks. Expect the bottom-left label to change from "vowel" to "talk", with a second blue
      line above it, and the value to show syllables a second (such as "5/s").
   2. Hold a key and turn blue left click by click until the value reads "off". Expect a steady held "ah" and a
      still mouth. Turn it right one click: expect slow babbling ("2/s"); each further click makes it faster, up to
      "10/s". Press blue again: the label goes back to "vowel".
   3. Play the same key several times, holding it about a second each. Expect the same "word" each time. Then play a
      neighbouring key: expect a different word.
   4. Press green (the label changes to "spread", a percentage that moves 10 a click) and turn it: low keeps the syllables close to one vowel, high makes them jump around. Press
      green again.
   5. Press white (the label changes to "chop", 10 a click) and turn it: low makes the syllables flow into each other, high makes them choppy. Press
      white again.
3. **Vowels.** With talk off ("talk" reads "off"), hold a key and turn blue slowly from fully left to fully
   right. Expect "ee" to slide through "eh" and "ah" to "oh" and "oo", with no clicks or crackle.
4. **Bodies.** Hold a key and turn green from fully left to fully right. Expect the same pitch to go from a very
   large, dark voice through a man, a woman and a child to a tiny, bright voice, and the label's value to read
   "giant", "man", "woman", "child", "tiny" in turn.
5. **Breath.** Turn white fully right. Expect a whisper with a faint pitch.
6. **Vibrato.** Hold a key with orange fully left: no vibrato. Halfway: a wide vibrato within a quarter second.
   Fully right: a fast warble. The label reads "vibrato" on the left half and "warble" on the right.
7. **Chords and range.** Hold a six-key chord. Expect no distortion or crackle, and each voice babbling on its
   own. Then play the lowest and highest keys over a few octaves. Expect no squeals or silence at either end.
8. **CPU.** Open the resource monitor (Shift + Help, then T1; turn blue until the bottom line starts with
   **2/6 synth**). Hold the six-key chord and note the "synth" number. Then turn blue to **5/6 pk**, press any knob
   to restart it, hold the chord for a few seconds, and note the "pk" number.

If the OP-1 freezes or restarts, note the step, then remove formant in the workshop.
